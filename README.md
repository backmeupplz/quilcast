# Quilcast

Quilcast is an open-source Farcaster client that runs in the browser, with apps for iPhone and Android. It talks straight to [Hypersnap](https://hypersnap.org), the Farcaster network that Quilibrium runs, rather than through a hosted API, and your signing keys stay in your own browser.

Reads hit the public node's HTTP API, writes are protobuf messages signed in the browser and posted to `/v1/submitMessage`, and keys live in the browser's IndexedDB, never in `localStorage`. The only server is a tiny static host that also gives shared links proper previews and fetches other sites' pictures (avatars, link previews, embeds and mini app icons) for you, so those sites never see your IP address; only our own files, Farcaster's image CDN and Tenor's GIFs load directly, along with Winamp skins from the Winamp Skin Museum when a profile uses one.

Live at **[quilcast.com](https://quilcast.com)**, and as an app for iPhone (App Store) and Android (Google Play, F-Droid, or the APK from the [latest release](https://github.com/backmeupplz/quilcast/releases/latest/download/quilcast.apk)).

## What it does

- Following and trending feeds (top-level casts), threads with their parent casts, profiles with separate casts and replies tabs
- Browse as someone else, signed in or not: a profile's ⋮ menu has "Browse as @name", which opens Home on that account's Following feed, built from who they follow (public data, no sign-in needed). It's read-only: nothing is signed as them, so likes, recasts and replies stay yours (or lead to Join when signed out), and your own mutes, blocks and spam filter still apply. A banner says whose feed it is, with Exit back to your own. The link (`/?as=<username or fid>`) can be shared, and the last five accounts you browsed as are offered again while browsing and on the signed-out home page
- Anonymous mode: without an account, Follow keeps people in a list on this device, and Home's Following tab shows their casts and recasts, newest first. Nothing is published. The browser merges each account's casts and recasts itself, since the node's multi-account feed loses older casts and has no recasts (`src/local.ts` has the tradeoff); following a few hundred accounts makes the first page slow, so imports stop once the list holds 300. Settings → Following on this device lists them, exports and imports the list (also from someone's public following list), and once you sign in offers, without doing it by itself, to follow them on Farcaster. Mutes and blocks work signed out too, kept on the device
- On a cast's page, the like, recast and quote counts open lists of who liked, recast or quoted it
- Notifications: grouped likes, recasts and follows plus replies and mentions, with an unread dot. Hypersnap leaves recasts out, so those are looked up for your 25 newest casts while the page is open
- Several accounts per browser, switched from the account menu (bottom left) or Settings
- Rich link previews (Open Graph and Farcaster mini app images) through `/api/unfurl`, and @mention autocomplete in the composer
- Search for people and for cast text. A profile's search button looks through that one account's casts and replies, newest first (`/u/<name>?q=<words>` links to it): the node's cast search can't be narrowed to an author, so the app reads the account's casts from the node's hub API, 1000 a request, and keeps the ones with every word in them
- Messages: farcaster.xyz direct casts. Inbox, Unread, Requests and Muted tabs, one-to-one and group chats (with faces and names, group events in words, images inline), muting, auto-delete, and a Message button on profiles. Updates arrive live over farcaster.xyz's websocket, so nothing polls, and conversations open instantly from memory. Direct casts aren't part of the Farcaster protocol, so the custody key signs farcaster.xyz's authorization once and Quilcast keeps a token that opens direct casts and nothing else. farcaster.xyz only answers its own site, so the server relays these calls without storing them. As on farcaster.xyz, they aren't end-to-end encrypted
- Spaces: farcaster.xyz's live audio rooms. Listen to what's live, see what's coming up and mark interest, or start one now or for later. Listeners raise a hand; hosts and co-hosts invite people up, move them back, make co-hosts and remove people. Reactions float over the room, and its chat is the replies to the Space's cast. Audio goes over LiveKit (`livekit-client`, loaded only for Spaces) and keeps playing while you browse, with a bar to get back. Space links in casts become cards you can listen from. farcaster.xyz only lets in accounts it has a key for (ones that have used farcaster.xyz or the Farcaster app), so accounts created in Quilcast see a note instead
- Wallet: create wallets or import them from a recovery phrase or private key in Settings (as many as you like), then switch between them from the Wallet tab, which shows the address, the balance across Base, Ethereum, Optimism, Arbitrum and Polygon, the tokens worth something, and NFTs (read from Blockscout's public explorers). Send to an address, an ENS name or a Farcaster @username (their verified address), and swap or bridge between those chains in one screen: LI.FI (keyless) compares DEX aggregators and bridges (Uniswap, KyberSwap, 1inch, Across, Stargate, Relay…) and the quote shows the route, the minimum received, fees and, for bridges, the arrival time, then tracks the transfer until it lands. Tokens are approved for exactly the amount, to LI.FI's contract only. Mini apps see these wallets next to any browser extension wallet; Quilcast shows each signature, typed-data request and transaction before it signs, and sends transactions through the chain's public RPC
- Cast, reply, quote, like, recast, follow, delete your own casts, post into a channel. A new cast shows as "Posting…" until the network has indexed it
- Attach up to four photos, videos or GIFs per cast (a quoted cast takes one of the four). GIFs come from Tenor's search (`/api/gifs`, trending until you type) and are embedded as Tenor's own `.gif`, which other Farcaster clients play too; nothing is uploaded. Photos are re-encoded in the browser (≤2048 px, JPEG, or PNG when they have transparency; no EXIF). Uploads go through `/api/upload`, signed by this browser's key. When the recovery phrase is in this browser, it also signs a 10-minute farcaster.xyz login, and the file goes to Farcaster's CDN the way Quorum does it. Farcaster only gives upload URLs to accounts that have a Warpcast signer, so other files (and uploads without that login, from wallet accounts for one) are stored on the server and served from `/m/<hash>`
- HLS video plays natively where the browser supports it and through hls.js (loaded on first play) in Firefox-based browsers
- Avatars and feed images load from Farcaster's image CDN at the size they're shown; the image viewer opens the original
- Create a new account: a recovery phrase is generated locally, you send about $0.20 of ETH on Optimism to it, and Quilcast registers the FID with `IdGateway.register`, adds a gasless Hypersnap signer (`KEY_ADD`) and claims your fname
- Sign in with an existing recovery phrase (the same derivation as Warpcast and Quorum, so phrases work across apps)
- Or use an external wallet (MetaMask, Rabby, OKX or whatever injects `window.ethereum`) as the custody address. The wallet signs the `KEY_ADD` (two EIP-712 signatures on chain 1) and the fname proof, and pays for registration on Optimism. Quilcast holds only the signer key it adds. viem drives the wallet and loads only when someone connects one
- Farcaster and Base mini apps: tapping a mini app's card in a cast opens the app inside Quilcast, and so does pasting its link under Mini apps, which keeps the apps you add and a Discover list (farcaster.xyz's top 100 mini apps through a cached `/api/apps`, with categories and search). Several can be open at once: one shows and the rest wait in the dock. So that they don't keep using memory and battery, an app left in the dock for 5 minutes goes to sleep, and so do the ones used longest ago when more than 3 wait there (1 on a phone, where every waiting app also sleeps when Quilcast goes to the background). A sleeping app's page is unloaded, and its dock icon is faded: tapping it loads the app again from the start. Apps showing a prompt, waiting on your wallet or allowed the camera and microphone stay awake. Pin favorites (from the list, the directory or an app's menu) and they stay in the dock even when closed. They get the full SDK host: context, sign in with your Farcaster account, compose a cast, view casts and profiles, open other mini apps, primary button, back, add to your apps, haptics (vibration on phones), camera and microphone (once you allow it per app), and token actions (send, swap through LI.FI, view). Your own browser wallets are their Ethereum provider (EIP-6963) and Solana provider (Wallet Standard). With several wallets (MetaMask, Rabby, Plain…) you pick one when an app first connects, and the wallet button in the app's header switches it in one tap. Each app keeps its own chain
- Mini app notifications: the Farcaster webhook flow. Turning them on gives the app a token for this browser's inbox on our server (`/api/notify`), handed over in a signed event our server delivers to the webhook in the app's manifest. Notifications show under Notifications → Mini apps and as Web Push (encrypted per RFC 8291, VAPID, no library). Apps check the event's signing key against Farcaster's KeyRegistry, which Hypersnap's gasless signers aren't in, so the first time you turn notifications on Quilcast adds one on-chain key from your custody on Optimism (a cent or less); signing out removes it
- Profile looks, MySpace style: your profile page can get a background (color, or a tiled or full-screen GIF), text, link and box colors, borders, corners, a font, a name effect (rainbow, glow, glitter…), a banner, a mood, a profile song (a YouTube video visitors press play on), About me and Who I'd like to meet, interests, a Top 8 of friends, a GIF wall, stickers you drag anywhere on the page (resize and turn them by the corner dot; each one sits on a box at a share of its size, so it lands on the same spot on a phone and a computer), and one of 27 moving effects (snow, rain, confetti, fireflies, fireworks, Matrix code, a sparkle cursor…, drawn by `src/fx.ts` on one canvas and skipped for visitors who ask for less motion), and a marquee: a line of plain text that scrolls across the profile at your speed, in your direction and colors, moved by CSS alone and standing still for visitors who ask for less motion. Six ready-made layouts get you started. GIFs come from Tenor's search through `/api/gifs`, or you upload your own. Looks are stored on our server (`/api/look/<fid>`), only change profile pages, and anyone can turn them off in Settings
- App themes: restyle all of Quilcast for yourself. A theme sets the colors (background, cards, lines, text, accent, likes, recasts…), a background picture or GIF (tiled, full screen or centered, with a scrim and a see-through column), pictures on the bars and buttons, corners from square to pill, border width and style, a glow, a font, the size of everything, a name effect on everyone's names, one of the 27 moving effects and stickers you drag anywhere on the screen. The editor previews the draft on the real app while you browse. Ten built-in themes, plus every theme in Quorum's gallery (`/api/quorum/skins`, relayed since it has no CORS). Share one as quilcast.com/theme/<id>: in a cast it shows a small Quilcast wearing it with an Apply theme button, and the link's share image draws the app in it. Themes move to and from Quorum: Quorum skin JSON (pasted or as a file) imports here, and "Copy for Quorum" / "Save for Quorum" make a skin Quorum's Skins > Import accepts (pictures become JPEGs inside the 2 MB budget; GIF motion, stickers, effects and name effects stay behind)
- Quorum mode (Settings, off by default): Quilcast laid out like Quilibrium's Quorum app. A narrow sidebar in Quorum's order (Home, Explore, Channels, Notifications, Messages, Spaces, Apps, Wallet, a "Create a post" button, then Settings, a sun and moon switch and your account card, which also opens your profile); Quorum's Governance, Saved posts and Status have nothing to open in Quilcast, so they're left out. Home gets Discover and Following tabs, a refresh button, a "What's on your mind?" composer and a line saying what the feed holds; the right column has "Search the network" (press `/`) and your apps. The wallet opens as a panel docked on the right: the primary wallet's total and address, sends and swaps still on their way, Swap, Send, Receive, Explorers and Permissions (revoke.cash), the networks, and Tokens, Collectibles and Activity (each chain's recent transactions from its explorer). A mini app floats over the feed beside the wallet rather than under it, and can grow to the page or be shared in a cast; below 1100 px the wallet slides over the page instead. Everything inside the frame follows Quorum too, from quorum-desktop's components and quorum-mobile's feed, launcher and wallet: Inter, Quorum's pill buttons, filled fields, modals, context menus and toasts; posts with 44 px faces and Like, Reply, Recast; Messages as two panes (the conversations beside the open one) with Quorum's message rows, day lines and pill composer; notification badges, sidebar-style lists and uppercase Settings sections; the mini app window's title bar with a search button that looks through the directory, and /apps as a grid of app cards. Phones get quorum-mobile's tab bar (your face, Messages, Spaces, Home, Notifications, and ⋮ for Wallet, Explore, Apps and Settings). With no theme on it wears Quorum's dark surfaces and purple accent, and themes recolor it like the rest; every Quorum rule is scoped to the mode (`src/quorum.test.ts`), so with it off nothing changes
- A profile Winamp: put YouTube videos and playlists on your profile and visitors play them in a Winamp drawn from any classic skin in the Winamp Skin Museum (our own renderer, about 7 KB), with the playlist, equalizer and shade modes
- The app speaks 22 languages: English, Arabic (right to left), Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Dutch, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal), Russian, Spanish, Swedish, Thai, Turkish, Ukrainian and Vietnamese. It follows the device's language unless you pick one in Settings; the strings are in `src/locales/` and `npm run i18n` checks every file has them all
- Translations: casts in another language get a Translate button (the language is guessed in the browser; translations come from free models through OpenRouter and are kept per cast)
- Mute or block people for a day, a week, a month or for good, and mute words. Farcaster has neither, so they're Quilcast's own, kept private on our server (signed with your key) so every device gets them. Report a cast or a person from the same ⋯ menu; casts and accounts taken down after a report go in `src/removed.json`
- Drafts: closing a cast with something new in it asks whether to save it as a draft, and the composer's Drafts list picks one up again (a reply reopens as a reply) on any device. Each draft is sealed with AES-GCM in the browser under a key made from the account's custody signature of a fixed message, so our server keeps files it can't read; a wallet account's wallet signs that message once per browser
- Quilcast is a Farcaster mini app itself: open quilcast.com links inside farcaster.xyz, the Base app or Quilcast
- Settings: profile (with photo upload), theme, light or dark, profile looks on or off, recovery phrase and key export, removing the recovery phrase from this browser, node and Optimism RPC URLs, version

- Channels: a Channels page (and a Channels tab in Search) lists farcaster.xyz's channels by followers, with search; a channel's page shows its picture, description, followers and members and its casts, newest first. Casts go under each channel's real parent URL (the oldest channels, like /memes and /base, aren't `…/~/channel/<id>`), and the composer warns before casting into a members-only channel you're not in. The directory comes from farcaster.xyz through the server (`server/channels.mjs`, kept an hour)
- RSS feeds: every account with a name has one at `/u/<name>/rss` (its casts, newest first; `?replies=1` adds its replies), and every channel at `/channel/<id>/rss`, under the channel's real parent URL. Profile and channel pages advertise them with `<link rel="alternate">`, so pasting a profile link into a reader finds the feed, and a profile's ⋮ menu has Copy RSS link. Each item links to the cast's page here and carries its text as the app draws it, with its pictures and links (`server/feed.mjs`). Feeds are built on the server and kept ten minutes, and readers that miss that cache share the profile pages' per-address limit; taken-down and nameless accounts have none

## How it compares

Compared as of November 2026, from each project's own site or repository:

- **The Farcaster app** (farcaster.xyz, formerly Warpcast) is the official client, on the web, iPhone and Android, and it is closed source. Direct casts and Spaces aren't part of the Farcaster protocol, so Quilcast reaches them through farcaster.xyz's own servers, and Spaces only let in accounts that farcaster.xyz has a key for.
- **[Herocast](https://github.com/hellno/herocast)** is also open source (AGPL-3.0) and runs in the browser. It is built as a power-user tool for people who run several accounts. Quilcast is MIT-licensed, aims at everyday reading and posting, and adds mini apps, a wallet and MySpace-style profile looks.
- **[Recaster](https://recaster.org)** offers scheduled casts, lists and bookmark folders. Quilcast has none of those yet.
- **[Terminus](https://testflight.apple.com/join/cSPBnkud)** is a native iPhone client for Hypersnap, in TestFlight. Like Terminus, Quilcast keeps your keys on your device and loads other sites' pictures through its own server so they can't see your IP address, and it also runs in any browser and on Android. Terminus sends the links you open through its proxy as well, whereas Quilcast opens them directly, without a referrer, after showing where a disguised link really goes.

Quilcast is a small project by one developer, and it depends on the Hypersnap node it reads from (`haatz.quilibrium.com` by default, changeable in Settings). Because Hypersnap doesn't report recasts in notifications, Quilcast only finds recasts of your 25 newest casts.

## FAQ

**Is there an open-source Farcaster client that runs in the browser?** Yes: Quilcast runs at [quilcast.com](https://quilcast.com) with nothing to install, and this repository is its full source under the MIT license. Herocast is another one.

**Do I need the official app to use it?** No. You can create a new Farcaster account inside Quilcast (it costs about $0.20 of ETH on Optimism), sign in with the recovery phrase of an existing account, or connect an Ethereum wallet that holds one.

**How do I know quilcast.com runs this code?** Every build's file hashes are published, and `scripts/verify.mjs` compares the live site with them. See [Verify this build](#verify-this-build).

**Can I run my own copy?** Yes. `npm run build && npm start` serves the whole app from your own machine, and it still talks to Hypersnap directly.

## Develop

```sh
npm install
PORT=3311 npm start   # API for uploads, after npm run build
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build && npm start   # production server on :3000
```

Your recovery phrase is kept in this browser, encrypted under a key the browser holds (see Security). Code running on the page, or anyone who copies your browser profile, could still use it, so treat it like a hot wallet, or remove it from the browser in Settings: posting, likes, follows and uploads only need this browser's signing key, and Quilcast asks for the phrase on the rare occasions the account itself has to sign (adding a key for this browser, signing in to a mini app, connecting messages or Spaces, notification keys). A phrase typed in then is used for that one action and not kept, not even in memory.

## Security

- Keys never leave the browser. Pages are served with a strict CSP (`script-src 'self'`, no inline styles, and only Farcaster clients may frame Quilcast) and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.
- Links in casts and previews are untrusted, because the network only checks their length. Only public http(s) URLs get rendered.
- The server's own fetches (link previews, card images, video posters) only reach public hosts on ports 80 and 443. Every address is checked at connect time and again on each redirect, and size, time and pixel limits apply.
- Messages use a token farcaster.xyz scopes to direct casts. The relay passes it to a fixed list of direct-cast endpoints and never stores or logs it, or the messages. Besides the relay, the page only connects to `wss://ws.farcaster.xyz` for live updates.
- Spaces use a farcaster.xyz session token the custody key signs for (kept with the account for 30 days, so a wallet signs about once a month). `/api/space` passes it to a fixed list of audio-room endpoints and never stores or logs it. Audio goes straight from the browser to LiveKit (`wss://*.livekit.cloud`) with the room token farcaster.xyz hands out, and live room events come over `wss://ws.farcaster.xyz`.
- Secrets never touch `localStorage` (`src/keys.ts`). Recovery phrases, wallet keys and tokens are encrypted with AES-GCM under a key the browser generates as non-extractable and keeps in IndexedDB. Farcaster signing keys (the browser's signer and the on-chain key for mini app notifications) are non-extractable Ed25519 keys themselves: the page can sign with them, but nothing, Quilcast included, can read them out, so they're also left out of the key export. A secret leaves `localStorage` (where versions before 0.22 kept everything) only once the vault has read it back. Code running on the page could still ask the browser to decrypt or sign, which is why the CSP allows no scripts but Quilcast's own; and browsers keep these keys in the profile on disk, so a copied profile can still open them. Adding a wallet shows a warning and needs an explicit tick
- Mini apps run in sandboxed iframes on their own origin (never Quilcast's, which holds the keys). They can't navigate the page, use the camera, microphone or location, or reach anything but a fixed list of SDK calls with checked arguments. Your wallet sees every request as coming from quilcast.com, so Quilcast does the checks it can't (`src/guard.ts`): an app sees a wallet only after you allow it, may only ask for signatures and transactions while it's on screen, can't use blind `eth_sign` or permission calls, can't get a Sign-In with Ethereum message for another site signed, and can't touch your Farcaster account (Farcaster typed data, transactions to Farcaster's contracts, Farcaster login messages). Sign in with Farcaster always asks first and builds the message itself. A Sign-In with Ethereum or Solana message for another site than the app's own is shown to you first; ones for farcaster.xyz, warpcast.com and quilcast.com are refused. Swaps may only call (and approve exactly the amount to) LI.FI's contract. The Quilcast wallet's prompts spell out token approvals and transfers (who may take what, and "unlimited"), Permit and Permit2 signatures and Seaport listings, and warn on them and on messages that aren't text; the yes button takes clicks only after a second, so an app can't time one onto it; and themes don't reach the prompts or the wallet's send and swap screens, so a theme's font or colors can't change what they say. Camera and microphone reach an app's frame only after you allow that app. The notification server knows no identities: an inbox is a hashed secret held by one browser, pushes only go to browsers' push services, and webhook events are relayed only when signed by the key they name
- Signers get only the permissions Quilcast uses (casts, reactions, follows, profile), and they revoke themselves on sign-out. Hypersnap keeps a signer alive as long as it's used, so Quilcast never renews one: only a key the network stops taking is replaced, which keeps the custody out of everyday use.
- A theme is settings too: `cleanTheme` (`src/shared.ts`) allows the same kinds of values as a look (colors, numbers in range, fixed lists, the same image hosts) plus a font only from our own media. Quorum skins are checked on our server: only base64 PNG and JPEG pictures whose bytes match their type, and TrueType or OpenType fonts, are kept, under their hash, and everything else in a skin is dropped by `cleanTheme`.
- A profile look is settings, never CSS or HTML. `cleanLook` (`src/shared.ts`) keeps only known fields: colors as `#rrggbb`, numbers in range, fonts, effects and layouts from fixed lists, text with length limits that's shown as text, and images only from Tenor, Farcaster's image CDN and our uploads. The server runs it before storing a look and the app again before showing one. Saving needs a signature from one of the account's Farcaster keys, as listed by the node. Every image is fetched once and refused over 6 MB for the background, 4 MB otherwise, or 25 megapixels. A song has to be a YouTube video that may be embedded, and it only plays when the visitor presses play
- Before upload, photos are re-encoded and MP4/MOV metadata (location, device) is wiped. Uploads stored on the server are checked against their claimed type and served inert (`sandbox` CSP). Each signed upload request works only once.

## Verify this build

Your keys are only as safe as the code quilcast.com sends your browser, so that code can be checked against this repository. The build is reproducible: a commit builds into the same files on any machine (a Mac's build matches the `Dockerfile`'s byte for byte), as long as Git checks the files out as they were committed, and `npm run build` ends by writing `dist/build.json` (`scripts/manifest.mjs`). It holds the SHA-256 of every file the server sends as it is, which is all of `dist/` except the HTML pages that the server fills in per page, and of the two inline scripts the pages may run, and the site serves it at [quilcast.com/build.json](https://quilcast.com/build.json). For each push to `main` that CI builds, it publishes that file to this repository's [`builds` branch](https://github.com/backmeupplz/quilcast/tree/builds) (`scripts/publish-build.sh`) as `<build>.json`, named by the build's id (the hash of its hashes, the id `/build.json` gives), with the first commit built into it, and adds `commits/<sha>` naming the build of that commit. The id is what the checker looks up. A push CI skips (one that changes only the native apps or docs, whose build is one already published) or cancels gets no `commits/<sha>` of its own. CI publishes the hashes whether or not that push passed its tests, so a published build says only what that commit builds into, not that it passed the tests or was deployed (the deploy builds its image separately, running the tests first, and stops if they fail).

The checker is a single file with no dependencies, so all it needs is Node 18 or later:

```sh
curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/backmeupplz/quilcast/main/scripts/verify.mjs && node verify.mjs
```

It reads which build quilcast.com says it runs, looks that build up among the ones CI published (on GitHub, falling back to git.borodutch.com), then fetches every file of the build from quilcast.com and compares each one byte for byte. It also reads the home page, a page of the app and About (add others with `--page /c/<hash>`) and checks that each script in them is one of the build's files or one of its inline scripts, that nothing else in them could load other code (a frame, a `<base>`, a refresh, or a header that prerenders or preloads another page or file), and that their Content-Security-Policy blocks scripts from any other site and any other inline script. It reads those pages strictly, by the exact forms of tag Quilcast's pages use, so a page written to read one way to the checker and another to a browser fails. It ends with the commit, so you can read exactly that code. If you'd rather trust no build but your own, build that commit yourself and check the site against it. The build itself needs Node 24 (the `Dockerfile`'s; 22.18 or later works too), because it runs TypeScript files directly:

```sh
git clone -c core.autocrlf=false https://github.com/backmeupplz/quilcast && cd quilcast && git checkout <commit>
npm ci && npm run build && node scripts/verify.mjs --dist dist
```

The `-c core.autocrlf=false` matters on Windows, where Git turns line endings into CRLF by default and so changes files the build copies as they are (the repository's `.gitattributes` turns that off too, but only for commits that have it). In a clone you already have, run `git config core.autocrlf false && git rm -rq --cached . && git reset -q --hard` first so that every file is checked out again as committed (it discards uncommitted changes).

A pass proves that quilcast.com just sent your computer exactly the code of that commit, in pages that load no other script. The security policy alone doesn't ensure that: it allows any script from quilcast.com itself, so it's the check of the pages that keeps out other files from the site. A pass can't prove what the server sends anyone else or at another time, because every visit fetches the code anew, so a server set on it could send the checker one thing and a browser another, or serve other pages than the ones checked. The check also leaves out the rest of the HTML and CSS the server draws around the scripts (titles, link previews, the signed-out home page's feed): under the policy those can't run code, but they could still change what a page shows or where its links lead. It covers quilcast.com only, not collect.quilcast.com. Files outside `/assets/` (but `/build.json`, which is never cached) may sit in Cloudflare's cache for up to an hour, so a check just after a deploy can fail on one of them until it's refreshed. The published hashes are what our own CI built, so only a build of your own is fully independent of us, and the surest way of all is to run Quilcast yourself: it's a small static app that talks to Hypersnap directly, and `npm run build && npm start` serves it from your own machine.

## Apps

The iPhone and Android apps are windows onto quilcast.com: a WKWebView (`ios/`, Swift, no dependencies) and an Android WebView (`android/`, Java, no dependencies, a ~30 KB APK). Web changes reach them the moment they're deployed, so a release is only needed when the apps themselves change. They open quilcast.com links (universal links and Android app links, from `public/.well-known/`), send every other link to the browser, ask for the microphone and camera only when Spaces or a mini app you allowed needs them, and tint the status bar to the page. The iPhone app asks people to accept the [Terms of Use](https://quilcast.com/terms) first. On an iPhone Duo, folding or unfolding the phone only resizes the web view, so the page keeps its state, and from 560px wide (the unfolded Duo is 585 to 669 points) the page has the sidebar instead of the tab bar; `ios/QuilcastTests` checks both on any simulator, and with `TEST_RUNNER_QUILCAST_SCREENSHOTS=<dir>` saves snapshots of each state.

```sh
cd android && ./gradlew assembleDebug        # app/build/outputs/apk/debug
open ios/Quilcast.xcodeproj                   # or ios/release.sh <version> <build> --no-upload
xcodebuild test -project ios/Quilcast.xcodeproj -scheme Quilcast -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17e'
```

One release ships everything: bump `versionName`/`versionCode` in `android/app/build.gradle.kts`, add `fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/changelogs/<versionCode>.txt`, and publish a release `vX.Y.Z` on git.borodutch.com. `.forgejo/workflows/release.yml` then uploads the bundle to Google Play, the iPhone app to App Review, attaches `quilcast-X.Y.Z.apk` and `quilcast.apk` to the release (here and on GitHub), and moves the F-Droid merge request to the new version. F-Droid builds the tag itself and ships our APK only when its build matches. Store texts live in `appstore/<locale>/listing.md` (the App Store's, with the screenshot captions; en-US is `appstore/listing.md`) and `fastlane/metadata/android/<language>/` (Play's and F-Droid's), in 24 languages, and `node scripts/store-copy.mjs` checks them against the stores' limits. The keyword research behind them is in Prime Radiant's `projects/quilcast/drafts/aso-<locale>.md`. CI builds the apps only when `android/` or `ios/` change.

## Contracts

Quilcast's contracts on Base live in `contracts/` and are built, tested, deployed and verified with [plain](https://getplain.org) (pinned in `contracts/package.json`; `cd contracts && npm ci && npm test`). Addresses are in `contracts/deployments/base.json`, and every contract is verified on Basescan, Sourcify and Blockscout. Both are owned by the @quilcast custody (0xad28…dd5d), which can only move their metadata.

- `QuilcastVote` ([0xe382…6704](https://basescan.org/address/0xe382921053c7c7d6dc1f831818034e829f626704)): the free "I voted for Quilcast" sticker (ERC-1155), one per wallet, minted from the [vote.quilcast.com](https://vote.quilcast.com) mini app (backmeupplz/quilcast-vote).
- `QuilcastRecede` ([0x23a7…5d8B](https://basescan.org/address/0x23a7Fb576e4fF5B987ac459d78A993F4C2585d8B)): the free "I receded my vote" sticker (ERC-1155), one per wallet, for fids holding farcaster.xyz's soulbound Network School vote token (which only farcaster.xyz can revoke), minted from the [recede.quilcast.com](https://recede.quilcast.com) mini app (backmeupplz/quilcast-vote, `recede/`).
- `QuilcastCollect` ([0x5961…0D3E](https://basescan.org/address/0x5961d6A95F140B40Cb993190E9dc5076f0830D3E)): every cast as a 1/1 ERC-721, collected once for free (gas aside) from the Quilcast Collect mini app that the gem button under each cast opens. The mini app is `collect/` (plain HTML and JS, its manifest signed by @quilcast), served by the same server when the host is collect.quilcast.com: `/c/<hash>` is a cast's page, whose link shows the NFT and who collected it (`/nft/0x<hash>.og.png`, and `.3x2.png` for the mini app embed), so a collector can share it. `node scripts/collect-art.mjs` redraws its icon, splash, embed and og images and the collection's. The token id is the cast's hash; its metadata and 1200×1200 picture are served by `server/index.mjs` at `/nft/0x<hash>` and `/nft/0x<hash>.png`, the collection's at `/nft/collection.json`, and a cast is kept on the data volume the first time it's asked for, so its NFT outlives a deleted cast.
